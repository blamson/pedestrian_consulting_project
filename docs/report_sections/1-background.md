Granby is a small mountain town (population ~2,000) on US Highway 40 in Grand County, Colorado. Highway 40 runs through town as Agate Avenue. This report quantifies how planned traffic improvements at two Agate intersections, Mesa Street and 4th Street, are expected to change pedestrian crash risk.

## Local context

Pedestrian crashes are rare in Grand County: CDOT records show **1 to 3 per year** countywide, and they are disproportionately severe. 2025 was a notably deadly year for the corridor, with **11 vehicle-vehicle traffic fatalities** on Highway 40 outside the Granby city limits. None involved pedestrians, but they raised local concern about traffic safety, particularly at the Agate Avenue crossings. The Mesa Street crossing is especially relevant because **three local schools (Granby Elementary, Middle, and High School) are within half a mile**, and students cross Agate to reach a convenience store and a coffee shop on the south side.

## Pedestrian crashes in Colorado

Statewide data reinforces the same pattern. From 2015 through 2025, pedestrians accounted for **1.1–1.7% of all reported crashes** in Colorado but **11.6–19.2% of fatalities** (CDOT Crash Dashboard). The fatality share has trended upward over the period, exceeding 17% in each of the past three years.

This framing matters for interpreting the results in this report. The before-and-after expected crash frequencies presented later are small absolute numbers, but the percentage reductions translate to meaningful gains in expected fatalities.

## The two projects

**Mesa & Agate: bulbout installation.** Bulbouts are curb extensions that narrow the roadway at a crossing. They shorten the pedestrian crossing distance, increase visibility between drivers and pedestrians, and slow turning vehicles. The project is justified in a 2025 SGM engineering study based on observed pedestrian volumes and proximity to the schools.

![SGM's proposed bulbout schematic for the Mesa & Agate intersection. The curb extensions shorten pedestrian crossing distance and improve driver-pedestrian sight lines.|600](docs/report_images/bulboutSchematic.png)

**4th & Agate: traffic signal and 6th Street diversion.** The town plans to install a signalized traffic light at 4th & Agate at an estimated cost of **\$986,000** and convert the 6th Street approach to right-turn-only. Drivers turning left from 6th currently experience long delays, and the diversion routes that traffic through the new 4th Street signal instead. The project is motivated by traffic flow, but it has direct pedestrian-safety consequences: 4th has a marked crossing with higher peak pedestrian volume than Mesa, and post-diversion vehicle volumes on 4th rise meaningfully.
