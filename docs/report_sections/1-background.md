## Local Context

Pedestrian crashes are rare in Grand County: CDOT records show 1 to 3 per year countywide, but they are disproportionately severe. 2025 was a notably deadly year for the corridor, with 11 vehicle-vehicle traffic fatalities on Highway 40 outside the Granby city limits. None involved pedestrians, but they raised local concern about traffic safety, particularly at the Agate Avenue crossings. The Mesa Street crossing is especially relevant because three local schools (Granby Elementary, Middle, and High School) are within half a mile, and students cross Agate to reach a convenience store and a coffee shop on the south side.

## Pedestrian Crashes in Colorado

Statewide data reinforces the same pattern. From 2015 through 2025, pedestrians accounted for 1.1–1.7% of all reported crashes** in Colorado but 11.6–19.2% of fatalities (CDOT Crash Dashboard). The fatality share has trended upward over the period, exceeding 17% in each of the past three years.

This framing matters for interpreting the results in this report. The before-and-after crash frequencies presented later are small absolute numbers, but the percentage reductions translate to meaningful gains in expected fatalities.

## The Two Projects

**Mesa & Agate: bulbout installation.** Bulbouts are curb extensions that narrow the roadway at a crossing. They shorten the pedestrian crossing distance, increase visibility between drivers and pedestrians, and slow turning vehicles. The project is justified in a 2025 SGM engineering study based on observed pedestrian volumes and proximity to the schools.

![SGM's proposed bulbout schematic for the Mesa & Agate intersection. The curb extensions shorten pedestrian crossing distance and improve driver-pedestrian sight lines.|600](docs/report_images/bulboutSchematic.png)

**4th & Agate: traffic signal and 6th Street diversion.** The town plans to install a traffic signal at 4th & Agate and convert the 6th Street approach to right-turn-only. Drivers turning left from 6th currently experience long delays, and the diversion routes that traffic through the new 4th Street signal instead. The project is motivated by traffic flow, but it has direct pedestrian-safety consequences: 4th has a marked crossing with higher peak pedestrian volume than Mesa, and post-diversion vehicle volumes on 4th rise meaningfully.

![4th & Agate prior to signal installation.|700](docs/report_images/forth.png)

## The Client's Problem

The Town of Granby is planning two traffic engineering projects on Agate Avenue. Both are costly, with the signal at 4th & Agate alone estimated at $986,000. The town asked whether the expected improvement in pedestrian safety from these projects could be quantified. For the Mesa bulbout, which is explicitly motivated by pedestrian safety near the schools, quantifying the benefit strengthens the justification. For the 4th Street signal, which is primarily a congestion-relief project, the question is whether it also delivers a pedestrian safety benefit despite increasing traffic volume at the intersection through the 6th Street diversion. The recent fatalities on Highway 40 have heightened local urgency around traffic safety on the corridor.
