## Executive Summary

Granby is investing in two traffic engineering projects along Agate Avenue. The first is a bulbout (curb extension) installation at Mesa Street, motivated directly by pedestrian safety concerns at a crossing located within half a mile of three local schools. The second is a $986,000 traffic signal at 4th Street, motivated by driver wait times rather than pedestrian safety, but which will alter pedestrian risk at the intersection because of the increased traffic volume diverted from 6th Street. This report quantifies the expected pedestrian safety effect of both projects using the federal Highway Safety Manual methodology.

> **Key Finding**
>
> Both projects are expected to meaningfully reduce pedestrian crash risk over a 10-year planning horizon, including the 4th Street signal whose primary purpose is congestion relief rather than pedestrian safety.

### Headline Results

| Intersection & Treatment | 10-Year Crash Probability | Reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Agate & Mesa, Bulbout | 15–20% → 10–13% | **33.3–35.0%** |
| 4th & Agate, Signal | 28% → 16–24% | **14.3–42.9%** |

### What This Means

- **Pedestrian crashes are rare but consequential.** Statewide, pedestrians account for only 1–2% of crashes but 12–19% of fatalities. Small absolute risk reductions translate to meaningful safety gains.
- **The Mesa bulbout shows a robust 33–35% reduction** in 10-year pedestrian crash probability, stable across our range of traffic volume assumptions for Mesa Street.
- **The 4th Street signal shows a 14–43% reduction**, depending on which estimation method is used. Both methods support the conclusion that the signal improves pedestrian safety despite increasing total traffic at the intersection, a useful secondary benefit beyond its primary congestion-relief purpose.
