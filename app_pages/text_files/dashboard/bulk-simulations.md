## Page Overview

This page runs large-scale simulations of long-term pedestrian crash outcomes across multiple scenarios. Instead of showing a single realization, it generates thousands of simulated time periods and summarizes the distribution of outcomes.

The result is a probability-based view of how often different crash counts occur over the selected time horizon.

## Why this is useful

Single-run simulations show what *could* happen. This view shows what *typically* happens.

By repeating the simulation many times, we approximate the full distribution of outcomes for each scenario. This makes it easier to distinguish between scenarios that differ meaningfully in risk versus those where differences are driven by randomness.

This is where this page differs from the previous page with single simulations. Clicking the simulation button a few times can show all sorts of results but doesn't give a good understanding of how rare those outcomes were. This one provides that insight by doing that experiment at scale. 

## How to interpret the results

Each bar represents how often a given number of crashes occurred over the simulated time period, expressed as a percentage of all trials.

* Taller bars indicate outcomes that are more likely for that scenario
* Pay careful attention to the scenarios with taller bars at 0, those are getting no accidents more than the other scenarios

The view is designed to support comparative risk assessment across scenarios under uncertainty, rather than simply providing obscure point estimates.
