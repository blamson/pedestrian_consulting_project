### Page Overview

This page shows the chance of pedestrian accidents over a long period of time. The previous page was focused on the rate of crashes per year, while this page looks longer time horizons such as decades. The crash rates have been translated to a percent chance of a crash as well, taking a different perspective on the results.

The plot on the left shows the percent chance of at least one crash for each scenario for the selected time horizon. The plot on the right shows how this chance changes as the time horizon moves through an entire century. 

### Why this is useful

A simple point value of expected crashes per year can be unintuitive to work with, especially in the context of this project when the pedestrian rates are far below 1. Looking at these from the perspective of a chance of occurrence over time is helpful, as we get a concrete idea of how likely these accidents are to happen in the future. We get to compare these across scenarios too, where different scenarios not only have different levels of risk but different degrees of growth as the time horizon increases. 

Safety improvements have benefits beyond immediate gains. Improvements now can result in serious gains to risk in the long term. 

### Assumptions

This page makes a very big assumption, that the rate of pedestrian crashes is **constant** across the selected time horizon. What this means is that we assume this rate does not increase or decrease at all, and pedestrian crashes are just as likely 50 years from now as in 3 years. 

We also assume that **traffic volume is constant** across the time horizon. A major AADT of 11,000 stays that way from year 1 to 100. This is an unrealistic assumption and was made for computational simplicity. In reality, the probability of a pedestrian accident would grow faster than shown due to the rise of traffic volume over time.
