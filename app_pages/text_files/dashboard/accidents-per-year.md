### Page Overview

This page presents the core model output: expected annual crash rates for pedestrians and vehicles at an intersection.

These rates are the fundamental unit of risk used throughout the analysis and serve as the basis for all downstream simulation and comparison.

#### Why this is useful

The values shown are *expected rates*, not forecasts of actual crashes in any given year.

A rate of 0.3 crashes/year does not imply a crash every ~3 years in a deterministic sense. It is the average of a random process: some years will have zero crashes, others may have multiple.


#### Interpreting Results

For interpretation, it is best viewed as a relative measure of risk across scenarios rather than a literal prediction.

When rates are below 1 (common for pedestrian crashes), a useful intuition is the implied time scale:

* 0.2 crashes/year ≈ 1 expected crash every 5 years (on average)

This is still an expectation, not a schedule of occurrence.

The primary use of these values is comparative: how interventions shift risk up or down across scenarios.


### Notes

Different countermeasures affect different components of risk. Some interventions (e.g., signalization) influence both pedestrian and vehicle crash rates, while others (e.g., bulbouts) primarily affect pedestrian exposure.

This reflects the structure of Crash Modification Factors: their effects are targeted, not uniform across all crash types.
